# Midway and Coral Sea: reviewed carrier cohorts

Review begun 21 September 2026, integrated 22 September 2026. This is a bounded comparison of aircraft carriers within two existing battles. Neither profile claims complete fleet or aircraft totals, exact tactical positions, or crew losses.

## Evidence and scope

For **Midway (Q173034), 4–7 June 1942**, the three US carriers are Enterprise, Hornet and Yorktown. The four carriers in **Nagumo’s carrier striking force** are Akagi, Kaga, Soryu and Hiryu. One US and four Japanese carriers were sunk or scuttled during that interval. The Japanese cohort excludes the distant main body’s Hosho; the US cohort excludes Saratoga, which arrived at Pearl Harbor on 6 June. Yorktown sank on 7 June. Hammann, Mikuma and other non-carriers are outside the denominator.

The [archived official naval overview](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/OnlineLibrary/photos/events/wwii-pac/midway/midway.htm), [preparations](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/OnlineLibrary/photos/events/wwii-pac/midway/mid-1.htm), [aftermath](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/OnlineLibrary/photos/events/wwii-pac/midway/mid-10.htm), and [official US Marine Corps history, volume I, pages 214–215](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USMC/I/USMC-I-V-2.html) were read in full. The latter distinguishes the named operational groups and their separation. Modern NHHC pages were corroborated through indexed passages when direct requests failed; these failures are not represented as successful document downloads.

For **Coral Sea (Q207165), 4–8 May 1942**, the US cohort contains Lexington and Yorktown; the Japanese cohort contains Shokaku, Zuikaku and Shoho. Lexington and Shoho supply one sunk/scuttled carrier per camp. Shoho belonged to a separate covering group and sank on 7 May, before the main carrier exchange of 8 May. The comparison covers the event interval; it does not depict a simultaneous surface engagement of two versus three carriers.

Sources read in full: the [archived official naval overview](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/OnlineLibrary/photos/events/wwii-pac/coralsea/coralsea.htm), [7 May action](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/OnlineLibrary/photos/events/wwii-pac/coralsea/cs-3.htm), and [8 May damage and Lexington’s loss](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/OnlineLibrary/photos/events/wwii-pac/coralsea/cs-5.htm). Yorktown and Shokaku are each retained as one damaged survivor in a non-renderable subset. These observations are not added to the sunk counts. Zuikaku’s aircraft depletion is not a lost ship. Operational-aircraft snapshots were considered but withheld because their scope differs from the entire event.

## Representation

Each event gains two military-unit carrier cohorts and two explicitly unquantified air components. Existing national participants and their source references remain as unquantified other naval formations, with names clarifying the complement. All original dates, coordinates, participant IDs and raw quantitative evidence are preserved. Midway retains the inherited direct-event point [-178, 30]; Coral Sea retains [162.33333333333, -16.266666666667]. This review does not independently certify those broad battle locators or place individual ships.

Carrier strengths and removals use the unit **ships**. Death arrays remain empty: a ship removal is not a crew-death estimate. Air and other naval formations have no population scale and no fabricated attrition. Both events remain partial profiles, since all represented formations cannot be quantified.

The new `ww2-straight-deck-carrier` is an explicit-only family silhouette for 1942 US and Imperial Japanese carrier cohorts. It has a straight full-length flight deck and no embedded aircraft. Its common starboard island does not reproduce Akagi/Hiryu’s port islands, Shoho’s islandless arrangement or Lexington’s exhaust structure. Aircraft are separate unquantified propeller-aircraft components. Geometry sources, costs and limitations are recorded in [the equipment audit](battle-cornwall-equipment-review-2026-09-21.md).

## Allocation correction

A fixed model allowance for unknown formations previously crowded out small known carrier cohorts under the mobile cap. Allocation now first reserves the documented reference density while retaining a visible place for unknown formations when the cap permits, then expands the illustrative unknown groups. The same rule applies to intermediate quotas across land, naval and air media. Unknown is not converted into a strength of ten. The ship/aircraft/personnel denominators remain separate.

Regression coverage checks Midway’s three/four carriers and one/four removals, Coral Sea’s two/three carriers and one/one removals, and mixed-media quotas. Desktop and mobile browser checks use literal independently specified counts, inspect both carrier and aircraft model requests, and assert zero inferred deaths. Final integrated test results and readiness measurements are recorded in the running implementation checkpoint.
