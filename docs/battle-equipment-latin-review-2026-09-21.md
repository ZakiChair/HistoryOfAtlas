## Havana identity addendum

The full profile is `records.Q1529375` in [battle-profiles.json](../data/curated/battle-profiles.json); the builder applies it before the equipment review. The replacement participant IDs are Q150981 (North German Confederation) and Q142 (France); side IDs are `north-german` and `french`. The German Empire did not exist at the event date, and the contemporary report explicitly calls Meteor North German.

1. [Hamburgischer Correspondent, 17 November 1870](https://img.sub.uni-hamburg.de/kitodo/PPN689065124_18701117/PDF/PPN689065124_18701117.pdf), Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek Hamburg: scanned contemporary report identifies the two opposing ships and their North German/French identity. The scanned PDF and extracted text were inspected. The source is **Hamburgischer Correspondent: Morgen-Zeitung der Börsen-Halle**, not Hamburger Nachrichten.
2. [Otago Witness, 31 December 1870](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/OW18701231.2.38), National Library of New Zealand: contemporary Havana account explicitly discusses Meteor screw entanglement, mast/rigging damage, Bouvet steam-system damage and return under sail. It supports Meteor screw-and-sail equipment and the duel identity. Its casualty statements disagree even within the report; none become quantitative data.
3. [Bouvet model, museum accession33 MG4, photograph by Rama](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bouvet-IMG_8732.jpg): photographed primary museum object, catalogued circa1865–1881 at Musée national de la Marine, Paris. The image was inspected at full resolution; visible stern propeller, single funnel and three sailing masts support a screw-and-sail sloop-family analogy. This is a visual observation of the period object, not a claim that the existing generic model exactly reconstructs the aviso. Commons metadata links the official museum record34193; that endpoint returned403, so the museum webpage itself is not represented as read. Photograph ©Rama, CC BY-SA3.0France; no photograph is added to product assets.

The reviewed profile keeps catalogue start1870-11-09 and coordinates[-82.358888888889,23.136666666667], their Havana-place provenance, total and unassigned arrays. A separate final quantity review uses the Otago Witness narrative to count one engaged vessel per side. The French mail steamer Nouveau Monde returned to port before the duel; Spanish Hernando Cortes accompanied as a neutral ship and signaled the end when Bouvet entered Spanish waters. Neither belongs in the opposing engaged-vessel counts. Both participant death/casualty arrays remain empty. No loss rate, crew ratio, tonnage ratio or equality of fighting power is inferred from the two-ship count. The 14-record pre-integration equipment comparison originally kept all numerical strengths unknown; this later, independently supported quantity review is included in the integrated profile.
