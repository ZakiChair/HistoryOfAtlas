## Corrections and unresolved details

- **J. R. Williams:** OHS dates the capture to **15 June 1864**; the imported entry says **10 May 1864**. The temporary records flag this without changing chronology. Neither sidewheel nor sternwheel layout was adequately established, so the transport receives no invented hull. This remains partial equipment coverage.
- **Foote action:** NHHC dates its Cardenas encounter to **23 April**, while the imported title/date is **25 April**. Equipment identity can be reviewed independently; the uncertain day remains explicit.
- **Hatsuse/Yashima:** [Q465283](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q465283) identifies the current Russian Federation navy, with modern country and institutional-successor statements. It is replaced, not upgraded. The new Russian local identifier describes the historical minelaying force. The minelayer is an illustration of the preceding operation; it must not be read as evidence that Amur exchanged fire with the Japanese battleships at the moment of the explosions. The Brazilian Navy account is an official secondary history; the 1908 USNI source is contemporary naval analysis, not an eyewitness log of this particular minefield.
- Contemporary publications can contain wrong dates, incomplete fleet totals, mistaken early loss reports, and partisan rhetoric. The Japanese General Staff translation has inconsistent years in its introduction; those years were not imported. No Ulsan totals, general Port Arthur fleet totals, or early Yashima fate report was promoted into quantitative data.
- A protected-cruiser miniature with shielded guns differs from Variag's documented unshielded arrangement; the record states this limitation. Likewise, Terror is a larger destroyer, and the armoured-cruiser family does not reconstruct Rurik's individual gun positions.
- The existing Port Arthur title is malformed (“Battle of Port peturth”). This batch does not silently rename it.
