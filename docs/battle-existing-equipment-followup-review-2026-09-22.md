## Scope and source criticism

The equipment drawings and historical windows were reviewed separately in the [Mexican](battle-mexican-equipment-review-2026-09-22.md) and [French Revolutionary](battle-french-revolution-equipment-review-2026-09-21.md) model reviews. The Mexican shako/blue-red tailcoat/light trousers and U.S. forage-cap/muzzle-loader remain clothing-family analogies. Neither outfit represents every regiment, militia unit, season or supply condition. The U.S. model does not claim an exact firing mechanism.

At Wattignies, the 1837 Lami painting supports later commemoration rather than contemporary uniform evidence. The existing cocked-hat profile uses contemporary Lesueur volunteers and Musée de l’Armée firearm evidence. Jourdan’s letter is partisan testimony; its praise and unquantified losses are not converted into numbers.

At Hanau, the shako model represents a French infantry component, not the Old Guard or Italian contingent. Marmont is retrospective firsthand testimony, not an impartial order of battle; its strength estimates, loss claims and evaluations are not adopted. The [municipal museum](https://www.museen-hanau.de/sammlung/objekt-der-woche/187-zeitzeugen) independently establishes the French imperial army and the 30–31 October engagement. The opposing Bavarian–Austrian aggregate stays unsplit and receives no blanket uniform.

Existing local participant identifiers, names, memberships and quantity observations are preserved. The pipeline appends review references to assigned participants and the event. Equipment references cannot supply strength or loss observations.
