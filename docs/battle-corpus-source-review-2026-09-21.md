# Quantitative battle corpus source review — 2026-09-21

The ABT and War Atlas sections are acquisition and evidence audits; neither corpus supplied automatically imported force or casualty counts. The separately reviewed CDB90 intake is documented below and in the [matching review](battle-cdb90-matching-review-2026-09-21.md). Seven ABT page snapshots (five initial examples plus two missing-data edge cases), publisher discovery files, and War Atlas exports are cached under `/tmp`; they are not production assets.

## American Battlefield Trust (ABT)

The publisher's [battle catalogue](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/battles) and [sitemap index](https://www.battlefields.org/sitemap.xml) provide discovery. The fetched index lists `sitemap.xml?page=1` and `sitemap.xml?page=2`; those child sitemaps were not crawled. [robots.txt](https://www.battlefields.org/robots.txt) excludes `/api/*`, `/export/*`, and search paths. The [terms](https://www.battlefields.org/about/accountability/terms-service), last updated October 2025, explicitly exclude automated extraction from the ordinary site-use licence (sections 5 and 10). This is not an openly licensed bulk dataset: a scalable acquisition should use a publisher-authorized export or agreement.

The facts are server-rendered HTML. Observed `drupal-settings-json` contains application configuration, not a documented battle-data API. Do not assume an unobserved JSON/API schema.

Observed extraction contract, if acquisition is authorized:

1. Scope the main `.battle-facts--single.battle-facts--fullview` to its own title/canonical event URL. Related-event cards must not be ingested as this event. Narrative and sidebar charts often duplicate values: deduplicate, compare, and reject disagreements.
2. Both strength and loss sections use `.battle-facts__casualties`. Distinguish them by the exact child title `Forces Engaged` or `Estimated Casualties`. The chart has `data-total`, `data-sidea`, `data-sideb`; cross-check them against visible `.battle-facts__casualties__chart__a`/`__b` labels and numbers. Preserve raw text.
3. In `.battle-facts--after`, each `.battle-facts__content__side` supplies title, total, and category text. **All three category elements use the same `__killed` class**, including wounded and missing/captured. Parse the visible category label, never the CSS class or a presumed array index. Missing and captured are combined, not separate additive values.
4. Require explicit event matching, historical opposing identities, reviewed count unit and scope, nonnegative finite values, and consistent arithmetic. A personnel casualty aggregate is not a death count. Never add categories on top of the aggregate or fill a missing side with zero.
5. All sampled chart values were integer strings; this inspection did not establish an ABT range encoding. Unknown, approximate, range, inequality, or unexpected strings must remain raw pending review. Do not derive exactness from chart formatting. The broader narrative may qualify apparently exact integers.
6. Preserve date precision: Gettysburg's sidebar shows July 1 while the page covers July 1–3; do not truncate the event to its first day.

For reproducibility, retain requested/final URL, status, retrieval timestamp, bytes, SHA-256, parser version, DOM field paths, raw strings, and all rejection reasons. Cache first and parse offline. A review manifest should explicitly approve identity, event scope, units, and numeric interpretation before any profile promotion.

## The War Atlas

The publisher offers direct [battles CSV](https://thewaratlas.co/downloads/battles.csv), [forces CSV](https://thewaratlas.co/downloads/battle_forces.csv), [casualties CSV](https://thewaratlas.co/downloads/battle_casualties.csv), [sources CSV](https://thewaratlas.co/downloads/sources.csv), [factions CSV](https://thewaratlas.co/downloads/factions.csv), and a [manifest](https://thewaratlas.co/downloads/manifest.json). The actual manifest states version `2026-07-03`, generated `2026-07-03T02:24:58.862887+00:00`, **CC BY 4.0**, attribution “The War Atlas — thewaratlas.co”; structured facts exclude Wikipedia prose. Its [methodology](https://thewaratlas.co/methodology) identifies Wikidata/Wikipedia/Commons upstreams and describes the atlas as an index rather than primary literature. The advertised JSON export returned HTTP 403 during this audit; no attempt was made to bypass that response.

There are 3,010 battle rows, all with QIDs, with years **1763–2026**; this export adds no ancient or medieval coverage. Of 19,020 bibliography entries, only **10 distinct generic collection entries** are referenced by any quantitative row. Gettysburg/Antietam link to the [NPS Civil War landing page](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/civilwar/index.htm), whose content does not substantiate those per-side figures. Other links include the NARA milestone-documents index and ABMC homepage. A nonempty source field therefore does not establish a number.

Force rows have only `battle_slug, side, strength_low, strength_high, confidence, source_slug`; casualty rows substitute `casualties_low, casualties_high`. They lack unit, force scope, category, original quotation, source page, or per-side entity key. Side names mix languages; the name is not a safe automatic join to historical factions. All 6,618 numeric rows share the same confidence label, which cannot serve as independent review evidence. There are 92 battle/side pairs with casualty minimum above force maximum; these require scope review, not automatic clamping.

A concrete semantic warning: Hampton Roads has a single US casualty range **108–261** and no strength rows. ABT's [Hampton Roads account](https://www.battlefields.org/learn/civil-war/battles/hampton-roads) instead labels 261 killed and 108 wounded. That numerical correspondence suggests category collapse into a range, although this audit did not inspect the War Atlas extraction code. Do not treat those bounds as established uncertainty. Gettysburg ranges likewise reference only a generic NPS collection.

Manifest SHA-256: `dba6e731990964ef5deab2fad0a170c49539966faa1b32559c8d24524a467d76`.

Recommendation: War Atlas's explicit reuse licence and QIDs make it a useful **candidate index**. Its current exports do not justify a mass promotion to reviewed quantitative profiles. ABT has richer labelled accounting but requires permissioned acquisition and field-level review. CDB90 is being evaluated separately against its original codebook as the more promising structured international intake; this audit does not pre-approve that alternative.

## Independent CDB90 numerical audit and adapter

Inspected original [CDB90 definitions](https://github.com/jrnold/CDB90/blob/e0b35ec4d798cec3bb4e6970c0f6bb7aa69e2f4c/src-data/M000121/CDB90DEF.csv), [original README](https://github.com/jrnold/CDB90/blob/e0b35ec4d798cec3bb4e6970c0f6bb7aa69e2f4c/src-data/M000121/README.TXT), [strength codes](https://github.com/jrnold/CDB90/blob/e0b35ec4d798cec3bb4e6970c0f6bb7aa69e2f4c/data/enum_codead.csv), and [conversion code](https://github.com/jrnold/CDB90/blob/e0b35ec4d798cec3bb4e6970c0f6bb7aa69e2f4c/create_data.R). The original README documents substantial disagreement between historical analysts and remaining database errors; the dataset should be attributed as one quantitative source, not certified ground truth.

`str` is military personnel strength, interpreted by `code`: 1 initial, 2 average daily, 3 total engaged. `cas` is personnel battle casualties, not deaths. Revised blank values are unknown; original -1 count sentinels are also unknown. Coalition `nam`/`actors` does not license allocating the side total separately to every coalition member. The adapter admits codes 1 and 3; differing codes require known initial strength plus reinforcements to reconcile with each side's reported total. It rejects code-1 contradictions with a known initial count.

Original note 11 defines the error percentages as adjudged possible deviations, not statistical confidence intervals. Minus fields are already signed: lower = floor(value × (100 + minusPercent) / 100); upper = ceil(value × (100 + plusPercent) / 100). Integer arithmetic avoids floating-point boundary errors. Each zero/blank percentage independently means an unknown bound and is omitted. Positive deviations can exceed 100% (observed strength +186%, casualties +280%). Original negative deviations are not generic missing sentinels.

Measured in the revised CSV: 1,320 sides; 986 code 1, 328 code 3, four code 2, two missing. All four deviation fields are deliberately blanked for 120 sides of rows 601–660 by the conversion script. Code-1 `str`/`intst` disagreements occur at row 338 attacker (1,940 vs 1,748), 437 defender (8,215 vs 6,653), and 443 defender (6,653 vs 11,343). Five sides have casualties above strength (rows 304, 319, 334); they cannot support direct depletion against that count.

There are 830 active periods for 660 battle rows, including 100 multi-period rows; do not repeat force totals per period. Thirty-six rows have a `parent` link for overlapping/subordinate engagements and must not be summed into the parent. The revised `datetime` is a midpoint, not the battle start. Unknown hours expand one known date to a midnight-to-next-midnight uncertainty window; month/year uncertainty is broader. Source periods must be checked against the whole mapped battle's dates. Two period rows (429, 435) lack valid start timestamps and require rejection or further source review.

`pipeline/battles/cdb90.py` provides a pure `build_profile` adapter for explicitly approved matches. Byte acquisition and hash verification remain the caller's responsibility. It preserves immutable revision URLs, source row/side/field IDs, original force names, raw error percentages, attribution, and unknown versus zero values. It never produces death counts or infers equipment. `pipeline/battles/test_cdb90.py` exercises arithmetic, coalition scope, identity, chronology, missing values, source corruption, incompatible strengths and repeat-application stability. No numeric row is approved merely because it exists in the source CSV.
