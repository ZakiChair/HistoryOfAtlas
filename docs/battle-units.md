# Authored battle miniatures

HistoryOfAtlas supplies 65 articulated GLB models across 70 equipment profiles, generated from original geometry by `scripts/generate-battle-models.py`. These are detailed, stylized miniatures: shaped bodies, faces, clothing seams, layered armour, equipment, harnesses, curved hulls, rigging and mechanical parts. They are not scans, photorealistic portraits, exact regimental uniforms or reconstructions of an entire historical army. Museum pages support equipment families and chronology; no museum image, texture or mesh is embedded in the files. Colours distinguish materials and formations and do not assert national uniform colours, flags or ethnicity.

## Selection and uncertainty

`lib/battles/units.ts` is the shared asset/eligibility registry. `data/curated/battle-profiles.json` and `data/curated/battle-equipment.json` own reviewed assignments to particular battles. Selection runs in this order:

1. An explicitly curated profile must match the medium, date and any required polity identifier. `documented-profile` means a sourced editorial choice of a representative equipment family, not verified equipment for every soldier.
2. A reviewed polity identifier plus its allowed date range can select a profile automatically, unless `explicitOnly` excludes the profile from both automatic selection routes. `UNIT_ANALOGIES` gives each reviewed army its own campaign window and source; generic asset eligibility never extends that window. This always remains `representative`; it does not establish troop composition, weapon proportions or an order of battle.
3. Unknown land equipment uses a plain, unarmed scale figure. A continent, event region, modern nationality or global timeline era never assigns a costume. Naval and air fallbacks use only the listed date-compatible technology silhouettes. If none fits, `dateCompatible: false` keeps the atlas marker without displaying a later machine.

Dates are conservative editorial eligibility windows for these particular assets, not claims about the invention or disappearance of a weapon. The registry uses the corpus’s astronomical year convention (0 = 1 BCE, −1 = 2 BCE); human-facing BCE labels are converted at display time. A year-only gate cannot settle an introduction within that year: the first combat use of tanks on **15 September 1916**, for example, still requires battle-specific source review before assigning a tank to a 1916 battle. Ancient armour and weapons varied within the same polity. Equipment can cross borders or be captured; only an explicit sourced assignment should override an automatic analogy.

Neither a profile nor its model determines numerical proportions. Strength, deaths and casualties remain separately sourced in the battle record. Human losses cannot be converted to lost ships or aircraft. An animated advance, weapon movement, fall or withdrawal is an illustrative presentation, not a claim about the exact timing, formation, location within the battlefield or mechanism of a historical death. Siege events should choose their documented infantry/artillery profile; a fortification, ram or siege engine is not invented merely from the event type.

The selected presentation keeps armies with unknown strength visible as groups of up to ten miniatures within the scene budget. These are explicitly labelled illustrative formations with unknown strength and no historical proportions. Their figure count has no population scale and does not establish an army size, troop mix or opposing camp. If the sources identify no armies, the neutral group makes no claim to identify one. A known force smaller than one figure's shared scale retains a single representative, with that resolution limit disclosed.

Compatible sourced losses also drive continuous percentage indicators through `armyLossShares`. Fallen and withdrawn miniatures use the separately rounded `unitLossState` poses, so a small loss percentage can appear without removing an entire figure. Neither missing losses nor an unchanged miniature count means zero historical losses. The progression of both indicators and poses remains illustrative.

## Runtime contract

- glTF 2.0 binary, metres, **+Y up, +Z forward**. Blender uses +Z up and −Y forward before the standard exporter conversion.
- Human soles are near Y=0; standing height is approximately 1.8 m, excluding tall weapons. Vehicles and ships use deliberately compact miniature dimensions, recorded in the registry. They are not literal full-size ship dimensions. Renderer bounds normalize ships/aircraft for legibility; spatial spacing must not be interpreted as an actual battlefield formation.
- Rigid articulated meshes, no skins or bones. Joint nodes retain names: `unit_root`, `hips`, `torso`, `head`, `arm_l/r`, `forearm_l/r`, `hand_l/r`, `leg_l/r`, `shin_l/r`. Riders prefix joints with `rider_`; horses and vehicles have named legs, wheels, turrets, oars or propellers.
- `March` is a looping movement clip; `Engage` is a separate illustrative action clip. Both animate rigid node transforms and have no translating root motion. The renderer places formations and can overlay a casualty pose. Firearm and crossbow Engage clips use small aimed recoil; bows have a draw-and-release gesture; melee troops retain a separate strike. Engage does not simulate historical ballistics or kill events.
- Opaque PBR materials only. No external textures, images, Draco/WASM decoder or network asset dependency. Each GLB embeds its geometry/materials/animations. Source geometry is cached; renderer instances each primitive across a formation and updates instance matrices from animated hidden hierarchies.
- NLA tracks use `HOLD` extrapolation. With `NOTHING`, Blender evaluated joint translations as zero outside the active clip, corrupting exported rest poses; the corrected files preserve rest hierarchy. Tests inspect actual standing/mounted joint locations and animated bounds.
- GLBs load only when battle mode needs their profile. `manifest.json` records bytes, triangle counts, mesh/node counts and clip names. The current full GLB catalogue is **21,946,748 bytes**, plus a **13,206-byte manifest**, for **21,959,954 bytes** total; individual scene requests use only their selected profiles. The catalogue growth guard is **22 MB** total, with fewer than 40,000 triangles per model. Both new twentieth-century naval models are below 500 KB individually. Each of the six specialist steam vessels is under 180 KB, so even two different specialist profiles require under 360 KB of model downloads.
