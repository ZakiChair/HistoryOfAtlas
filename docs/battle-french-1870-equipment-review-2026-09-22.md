# French line infantry equipment review — 22 September 2026

The original `french-line-infantry-1870` asset represents one French line-infantry component with a campaign capote, low képi and Chassepot M1866. It is limited to **1870, land, explicit assignments**, with historical equipment identities **Q71092 (Second French Empire)** or **Q70802 (French Third Republic)**. Both automatic selection paths skip this profile. Modern France, the First Empire, the First Republic, unknown local identities, 1869 and 1871 are ineligible.

The five integrated assignments concern existing quantified CDB90 forces at Wissembourg, Spicheren, Mars-la-Tour, Gravelotte/Saint-Privat and Coulmiers. Their local participant IDs, names and mixed-army totals remain intact. These figures do not imply that cavalry, artillery, zouaves, tirailleurs, guards, mobiles or francs-tireurs wore line-infantry dress. The underlying renderer uses a representative component to illustrate the aggregate; it does not know the numerical composition of those armies.

## Evidence for the silhouette

- [Musée Guerre et Paix en Ardennes, sequence 2](https://www.guerreetpaix.fr/sequence-2) specifically describes the French infantryman of 1870 with garance trousers, a blue-grey capote and képi. This supplies the campaign clothing context. It does not establish one uniform for every regiment or every battlefield participant.
- [Musée de l’Armée, Second Empire uniform panel](https://www.musee-armee.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/Documents/Support-panneaux-jeune/NAPIII_PanneauxJeunes-uniforme_BAT.pdf) illustrates a surviving sergeant-major’s uniform of the 28th Infantry. The inspected object provides the red cloth cap with dark band and visor, straight red trousers, short gaiters and equipment context. The model omits his rank braid, regiment number, epaulettes and distinctive tunic details. A noncommissioned officer’s object is not presented as a universal private’s complete uniform.
- [Musée de l’Armée, Chassepot object sheet](https://www.musee-armee.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/Documents/Support-Visite-Fiches-Objets/Fiches-1815-1870/MA_fiche-chassepot.pdf), inventory M2765, illustrates the firearm and open breech. The museum establishes its single-shot needle action, paper cartridge, 1866 adoption and use during the 1870–1871 war. The 1.31 m rifle and 1.88 m length with bayonet guide the model’s proportions. Its wooden stock, steel barrel bands, straight bolt handle, rear cocking piece, cleaning rod and brass-handled sabre bayonet are distinct from the older flintlock families and later magazine rifles.
- [Paris Musées / Carnavalet, Draner, _Infanterie de ligne, régiment de marche_](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/les-soldats-de-la-republique-3-infanterie-de-ligne-regiment-de-marche) is a **1871** print, inspected through the museum’s IIIF image. It corroborates the campaign silhouette: soft cap, capote with front skirts turned back, red trousers and carried equipment. The print is not relabelled 1870 and does not extend eligibility to 1871. Its illustrative colours and the museum objects are not treated as colour-calibrated measurements.

This is an original simplified geometry, not a scanned museum object. The capote is one attested campaign arrangement; clothing condition, facing colours, supply, weather and branch varied. The model does not copy the artist’s scarf or infer a regiment from the illustration. Nor does it borrow the later M1877 capote or a 1914 soldier’s equipment. The under-barrel rod is a cleaning rod, not evidence for muzzle loading. The curved flat yataghan blade and hooked quillon distinguish the Chassepot’s sabre bayonet from a triangular socket spike.

The distinction between the August imperial armies and November republican army is an explicit event review. The resolver only knows the year, so eligibility alone cannot determine whether a 1870 event preceded the September change of regime. No automatic rule is added. The CDB label “Army of Lorraine” is retained even where institutional sources call Bazaine’s force the Army of the Rhine.

No source’s strength or casualty statement is imported by this review. In particular, the loss claims in Lanquetin’s letter are not adopted. The Wissembourg monument inventory notes that it found no corresponding Curtelin entry in the town death register; the evidence used here is the inscription’s regimental identification, corroborated by the museum testimony, not a new casualty observation.
