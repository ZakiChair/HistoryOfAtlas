# Japanese winter infantry equipment review — 21 September 2026

The new `japanese-russo-war-winter-infantry` profile illustrates a Japanese infantry component in the winter campaign of the Russo-Japanese War. It combines a documented cloth service cap, drab wool overcoat, canvas leg wrappings and a Type 30 rifle with knife bayonet. It is a seasonal equipment analogy, not a reconstruction of every soldier at Mukden.

## Eligibility and Mukden assignment

The date window is **1904–1905**, restricted to the reviewed war. The eligible historical polity is **Empire of Japan, `Q188712`**. This is not modern Japan `Q17`, the Tokugawa shogunate or an identity inferred from the battlefield's location.

The profile has `explicitOnly: true`. Both automatic paths—the reviewed participant/date analogy list and the general polity match—reject it. This matters because the resolver currently receives a year, not a seasonal date. Even an accidentally added future analogy rule cannot automatically dress a summer battle in the winter coat. An explicit assignment must still pass the year, medium and historical identity checks.

The integrated equipment-only review assigns the profile to `Q384091:japanese-manchurian-army` at **Mukden, 19 February–10 March 1905**. [JACAR's battle account](https://www.jacar.go.jp/exhibition/nichiro2/sensoushi/rikujou09_detail.html) identifies the Japanese Manchurian Army, its component armies and the February–March campaign. Its separate `equipmentIdentity` supports the Empire of Japan affiliation without replacing the local army ID or collapsing the component armies into a new invented quantity.

Applying the Japanese proposal to the published record was checked against the actual resolver before integration. At that checkpoint the Japanese participant resolved to the new profile, while `Q384091:russian-manchurian-armies` remained unclassified. The Japanese addition alone supplied **one partial equipment improvement**. The subsequent [Russian winter equipment review](battle-russian-equipment-review-2026-09-21.md) supplies the Russian aggregate with a separate Mosin 1891 profile, making both displayed components classified. Names, IDs, camps, dates and every strength, death and casualty claim remain unchanged; neither assignment reconstructs the whole historical army. There are **zero automatic assignments**. The Port Arthur siege beginning in August 1904 was not assigned this winter variant; records without participants remain without invented armies.

## Authored model and validation

All geometry is original and produced by `scripts/generate-battle-models.py`. The cap, winter overcoat and Type 30 rifle have distinct named groups. The rifle is approximately 1.28 m before the fixed 0.40 m knife blade. Its bolt, internal magazine floorplate and rear hooked cocking piece are separate from the existing Mauser and Martini-Henry silhouettes. It has no steel helmet, detachable box magazine, Martini underlever or socket spike bayonet. The cap and coat retain material colours in the rendered scene; camp colour belongs to the surrounding formation UI.

The equipment remains stylized: coat tailoring, glove detail, exact dye shades and individual markings are simplified. Winter head covers, some underlayers, packs, regimental distinctions and varied issue equipment are not exhaustively represented. March and Engage show movement and recoil, not a complete bolt-operation drill or evidence of actual battlefield tactics. The long coat is a single articulated torso garment, not a cloth simulation.
