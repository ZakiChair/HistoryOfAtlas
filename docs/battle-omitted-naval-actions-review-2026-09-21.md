## Cornwall and Pinguin, 8 May 1941

The [Australian War Memorial's photograph 128072](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C48238) identifies Pinguin's sinking by HMS Cornwall and the date. The [British Naval Intelligence Division report CB4051(29), October 1941, pp.20–21](https://w.uboatarchive.net/Int/PinguinINT.htm), read in an archival transcription, describes the surface action, Cornwall's damage and Pinguin's destruction. It distinguishes the previously departed Adjutant and the distant supply vessel Alstertor from the encounter.

The profile compares **one surface ship per side**, with **zero British and one German ship removed**. Cornwall's reconnaissance aircraft assisted the search; they are explicitly outside the ship denominator. The quantities do not claim complete aerial coverage, equal fighting power, zero British damage or zero human casualties. No personnel figure or prisoner estimate is imported. The intelligence report itself warns about unverified prisoner statements; its detailed damage, tactical and human-loss claims are not certified by this review.

The raw event date and **[57.8, 3.5]** locator remain unchanged. The coordinate is not promoted to an independently surveyed point. An Admiralty war-diary search extract presented an ambiguous OCR latitude; the original May PDF could not be retrieved (DNS failure on the historic CDN, HTTP 403 on alternative official delivery hosts, web-reader failure). That extract was not used for a coordinate correction. No successful local cache of that PDF is claimed.

No specific heavy-cruiser or armed-merchant-raider equipment profile is assigned. The existing date-based naval silhouette is generic and does not reconstruct either ship.

## Successive captures near James Island, 1813

[NHHC DANFS, Georgiana](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/g/georgiana-ship.html), dates the captures of Catherine, Rose and Hector to **28 May 1813**. [Porter's 1815 journal, volume I, pp.210–212](https://whalesite.org/galapagos/1815%20-%20Porter%20-%20Journal%20of%20a%20cruise%20made%20to%20the%20Pacific%20-%20vol%201.htm), reports Downes's account at their later reunion and identifies the three armed whalers and their capture sequence. The journal is a primary account reproduced in transcription; its later narration is not used as an independent exact-day observation.

The profile compares **Georgiana with three successively encountered vessels**, not simultaneous three-to-one gunfire. Catherine and Rose did not resist; Hector fought. All three were captured, after which Rose was disarmed and released to carry paroled prisoners. The **zero/three removals** therefore denote the action's captures and loss of fighting availability, not three sinkings or permanent American possession. The note makes the sequential scope explicit. Essex was absent from the action and is not added to the American denominator.

The inherited 28 May date follows DANFS. Its account distinguishes reunion with Essex on 24 June. A separate NHHC campaign overview's prize list associates some captures with the reunion date; that is not used to redate this encounter. No precise offshore location was established, so the fiche remains **unmapped**, with its sourced quantities readable. James Island's center is not substituted.

## Conventional “Charles Island” action, July 1813

[Porter, pp.215–217](https://whalesite.org/galapagos/1815%20-%20Porter%20-%20Journal%20of%20a%20cruise%20made%20to%20the%20Pacific%20-%20vol%201.htm), places Essex, Greenwich and Georgiana in pursuit of Charlton, Seringapatam and New Zealander. Greenwich and Seringapatam exchange broadsides; Essex pursues and captures vessels while Georgiana supports the American force. Three ships on either side are a count of the whole pursuit, not six ships of equivalent firepower firing simultaneously. All British vessels are captured; Charlton is later disarmed and released with prisoners. The profile records **zero/three operational removals**, with no inferred human deaths.

The raw **14 July** date is retained as a disclosed inference from Porter's sequence: Charles Island on the 12th, the following morning's departure, overnight passage, then the encounter after daylight. DANFS gives **13 July**. The one-day disagreement remains explicit in the record; this review does not fabricate an interval or claim that independent sources agree on an exact day.

The conventional title does not establish the fighting position. Porter places the encounter off Banks Bay after leaving Charles Island; the island center would be misleading. No precise coordinate is assigned, and this fiche also remains **unmapped**.
