# Russian winter infantry equipment review — 21 September 2026

This review adds one original model, `russian-russo-war-winter-infantry`, for a representative Russian infantry component in winter field dress during the Russo-Japanese War. Its registry window is **1904–1905**, its historical polity is the **Russian Empire (`Q34266`)**, and its medium is **land**. It requires an explicit, reviewed event assignment. Neither a date nor the Russian Empire identity selects it automatically, including through an analogy rule.

The integrated assignment is the existing aggregate `Q384091:russian-manchurian-armies` at Mukden. This is an equipment illustration, not an assertion that all 310,000 men were infantry, carried identical rifles or wore the same winter clothing. The model is not extended to the First World War or to summer battles in 1904–1905.

It was not used as proof of universal papakha use or of winter dress. The construction combines attested features into an explicitly representative winter figure; it does not claim to reproduce a named sitter or regiment.

## Authored geometry

The model has a long grey-brown coat, a low irregular black fur hat, thick felt boots, cloth-covered hands, a folded bashlyk and plain leather cartridge pouches. The coat has a plain overlap; no invented regimental numbers, rank badges or cavalry button arrangement were added. Colour values are approximate material choices, not measurements of surviving dye.

The full-length Mosin differs from the Japanese Type 30: its integral magazine projects below the stock, its cocking piece is round, and its bayonet is an offset socket with a cruciform spike. It has no hooked Japanese cocking piece, knife-bayonet grip, steel helmet or optical sight. Small mechanisms and fur are simplified. The original procedural mesh uses no externally downloaded geometry or textures.

The Blender preview was inspected at `/tmp/historyofatlas-russian-winter-preview.png`. The long coat, dark fur hat, felt boots and projecting rifle magazine read distinctly from the Japanese winter model. The thin bayonet and small sight are less visible at ordinary battle-scene scale. The preview is a controlled render; browser and mobile checks belong to the subsequent integrated build.
