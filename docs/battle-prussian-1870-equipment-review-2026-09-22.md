# Prussian line-infantry component review — 22 September 2026

The new `prussian-line-infantry-1870` profile depicts a representative Prussian infantry component with a leather spiked helmet, blue tunic, grey trousers and Dreyse needle rifle. It requires an explicit assignment, land combat, the year **1870**, and equipment identity **Q27306, Kingdom of Prussia**. It is not selected automatically, including through an analogy rule. Modern Germany, the German Empire, the North German Confederation as a whole, and Bavaria do not satisfy that identity gate.

The four equipment assignments are integrated in the curated and public records of the existing quantified CDB90 army aggregates, as confirmed on 23 September. These are equipment analogies, not a uniform or weapon inventory for every person in those armies. The French opponents retain their existing equipment. Coulmiers is excluded: the Bavarian force there is not converted into Prussian infantry.

## Object and visual evidence

- [Musée Bonaparte, Auxonne / Ministry of Culture POP, 2010.0.72](https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/01320016844): a leather-and-brass helmet captured from the 34th Pomeranian Fusiliers in November 1870. The front and side photographs were inspected. They establish the rounded dark leather shell, projecting visor and neck guard, brass spike on a cruciform base and winged front plate. This is an actual wartime object, but not one proven to have been at the four August battles. Its special regimental scroll and inscriptions are omitted from the original model. The incomplete surviving chin furniture is represented schematically.
- [Deutsches Historisches Museum, Focus Königgrätz](https://www.dhm.de/en/exhibitions/archive/2016/focus-koeniggraetz/): the inspected photograph explicitly identifies the Prussian infantry helmet on the right as M1860. It corroborates the leather shell, cruciform spike base and visor before the 1870 campaign. The Austrian shako beside it is excluded. A separate [DHM M1867 line-infantry record, U1254](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/WFPQA7Q6XFM4X6ZX2ITWFIG3SBZC6NG6), confirms that multiple period patterns must be distinguished; its image was not accessible. The GLB is consequently described as a period family, not an exact M1867 reconstruction.
- [Musée de la Bataille du 6 août 1870, Woerth, BATW.1987.1.1](https://www.museesgrandest.org/les-collections/fusil-dreyse-modele-1862/): the inspected Dreyse M1862 photograph and catalogue description establish a 1.355 m rifle, wooden stock, three brass bands, straight bolt handle, rear cocking sleeve, sight and cleaning rod. The model has the visible brass finger spur behind the trigger guard. These distinguish it from the Chassepot and from later magazine rifles. The [Musée de la Princerie’s 1870 collection](https://www.musee-princerie-verdun.fr/explorer/collections-permanentes/militaria/) independently places Dreyse and Chassepot weapons in the war’s material context. No fixed bayonet is modeled because this bounded review did not verify its specific type and configuration; omission is not evidence that the troops lacked bayonets.
- [Paris Musées / Musée Carnavalet, G. Richard campaign-uniform print](https://parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/tenue-de-campagne-des-armees-prusiennes-badoise-bavaroise), image manifest `320154347`: the inspected color lithograph distinguishes the line soldier from guards, riflemen, cavalry, Baden and Bavaria. Its line-infantry figure supplies the blue tunic, red collar and cuffs, grey trousers with narrow red piping, light carrying straps and havresac. The same sheet shows greatcoats and other dress. The museum places it in its 1870–1871 war series; its item page does not provide a precise publication day. It is visual evidence for a representative combination, not proof of every regiment’s issue, of an exact color under every condition, or of a soldier at one named battle.

The eligibility window is deliberately restricted to the reviewed 1870 cases. It is not a claim that these objects originated in 1870 or disappeared in 1871. No field-grey 1914 tunic, later steel helmet, guard star, rank insignia or numbered shoulder strap has been imported into this family.

The institutional biographical entries establish affiliation and participation; they do not establish the illustrated dress. Object and print sources establish the equipment analogy separately. No infantry share, troop subdivision, new participant, strength or loss is inferred. Original local IDs, names and coalition membership remain intact. The appearance represents one evidenced component even when the existing scene uses it for the aggregate’s figurines.

## Implementation, cost and verification

Only the registry, procedural generator, unit tests, new GLB, model manifest and this audit were authored for the model. All geometry is original; no museum photograph, mesh or texture is embedded in the GLB. Only the new asset was generated.

The optional 22.5 MB allowance was not needed. The 64 existing GLBs remain byte-for-byte identical to `/tmp/historyofatlas-prussian-alias-baseline/models.json`. Lazy scene loading remains unchanged. The new model SHA-256 is `3566d50d799c68a1ad2fd78dfafd0f4bb22b31d7e95cc8b72963cb395bd4f61a`.

Two tests failed first because the profile and asset did not exist. After implementation, 44 unit and equipment-identity tests passed, as did TypeScript and targeted ESLint. Tests cover explicit and automatic selection paths, years outside 1870, wrong identities and media, reviewed local identity, the Dreyse’s full length and three bands, helmet proportions, absence of later weapon parts, complete geometry, animation bounds and forward firing/recoil. `March` and `Engage` are present. The rendered preview was inspected; the spike was shortened to match the period object’s proportions. Fine insignia and mechanisms remain simplified at the miniature’s viewing scale.

The proposal `/tmp/historyofatlas-prussian-1870-equipment-proposal.json` contains only the four participant equipment assignments and their sources/notes. Schema validation and application in memory against the supplied baseline resolve all four from `unclassified-unit` to the new profile while preserving every opponent, identity, date, coordinate, quantitative observation and pre-existing source. Reports are `/tmp/historyofatlas-prussian-1870-equipment-validation.json` and `/tmp/historyofatlas-prussian-1870-budget-validation.json`; the inspected preview is `/tmp/historyofatlas-prussian-1870-preview.png`. Integration and browser verification are performed separately by the parent task.
