# Battle catalog and animation evidence

The catalog includes every validated battle, siege and naval battle from the published atlas, then audits every additional cached candidate in the same Wikidata class closure. Missing dates and locations remain missing; excluded fictional, legendary and hypothetical events remain excluded.

```sh
python3 pipeline/battles/acquire.py
pnpm exec tsx pipeline/battles/build.ts
pnpm exec tsx pipeline/battles/check.ts
pnpm exec tsx pipeline/battles/readiness.ts
```

Acquisition resumes from `data/raw/wikidata`, using the existing rate-limited API client and raw response metadata. It fetches all candidate QIDs and explicitly linked P276 places and P710 participants, then requests original-language labels for candidates with no English or French name. It does not modify candidate discovery or rewrite the published event corpus. To refresh discovery itself use the main acquisition command; it now paginates deterministically.

New candidate downloads retain all source-language labels and descriptions. For an older EN/FR cache, acquisition also retrieves the full language response for offshore land rejections and additional mapped records, skipping completed full-language downloads. Explicit engagement descriptions (for example French `engagement naval`, Italian `battaglia navale`, or English `air battle`) can identify a medium without inferring one from the event name or its parent war. These descriptions retain the event's source revision.

Additional battle coordinates identify their exact Wikidata P625 statement. A directly classified siege can retry a later source coordinate or an explicit P276 place if the first point fails the land check. Generic battles cannot silently move an offshore position to a nearby city, because their apparent land classification may be incomplete. Broad water and island regions (oceans, seas, gulfs, bays, straits, and archipelagos) cannot supply their central coordinates as a battlefield. Existing published atlas records remain unchanged by this additional-candidate resolver.

The builder writes only `public/data/battles`. `index.json` is loaded on demand; `events/{QID}.json` contains each complete battle record. `coverage.json` reports missing candidate downloads, catalog coverage, exclusions and reasons why records cannot be mapped. A complete acquisition is not a claim of exhaustive historical coverage.

## Quantities

Raw P1132, P1120 and P1590 statements retain bounds, qualifiers, statement IDs and references. P1132 may count people or groups, so a raw number does not automatically represent soldiers. P710 may identify a commander rather than an army. P1120 counts deaths; P1590 counts deaths and injuries. A raw scoped figure is never promoted to an exact total, and a total is never split into invented sides.

Only editorially reviewed quantities with `renderable: true` may scale military representations. The count unit must match the represented unit: human casualties cannot remove naval vessels. Deaths and casualties overlap and must never be added together. `note` retains the source's loss definition, including missing or captured personnel when applicable.

The chosen presentation keeps unknown-strength armies visible as illustrative groups of up to ten figures, subject to the scene budget. These groups have no `soldiersPerModel` scale: equal group sizes do not imply equal historical forces, and no strength, losses, equipment or opposing camp is inferred from their appearance. A record without identified armies uses a neutral illustrative group. The interface identifies these formations as unknown-strength illustrations without historical proportions. Reviewed strengths share a population scale; a positive force smaller than one figure retains one representative, with the rounding limit disclosed.

`armyLossShares` preserves continuous percentages from compatible reviewed losses. Whole-figure poses use `unitLossState` and round to the scene's miniature resolution, so a small nonzero percentage can remain visible numerically without a fallen or withdrawn figure. Both presentations use an illustrative time progression; neither establishes the historical sequence of casualties.

`data/curated/battle-profiles.json` provides manual reviews of participants, opposing camps, and, where available, per-side figures and representative asset selections. The separate `battle-cdb90-profiles.json` contains reviewed imports from the CAA Database of Battles, Version 1990. `profiles.ts` validates both files and gives manual reviews priority. Reviewing an identity does not require known quantities or equipment. `coverage.json` lists every participant review in `reviewedParticipantProfiles`; `quantitativeEvidence.reviewedBattles` contains only the subset with comparable opposing strengths. Coalition participants use local IDs when one source count covers several states. Original Wikidata provenance remains attached to the record. Numbers link to their historical data publication, museum, battlefield authority, primary account or university source. Estimates retain that status; display midpoints are explicitly identified. Historical movement, formations and uniforms are illustrative unless separately documented.

### Reproducing the CDB90 import

```sh
pnpm data:battles:cdb90 --fetch
pnpm data:battles
pnpm data:battles:cdb90 --check
pnpm data:battles:check
```

The source manifest pins Jeffrey B. Arnold's revised database to an immutable commit and verifies the bytes and SHA-256 of all nine source/definition files. `--fetch` downloads only missing files into `data/raw/cdb90`; ordinary site builds consume the committed generated profiles without network access. The original CAA data is public domain; the revised database is ODC-BY, with attribution in every imported record and the public methodology page.

The [reviewed crosswalk](../../data/curated/battle-cdb90-matches.json) approves individual event/force identities, not fuzzy name matches. The [matching review](../../docs/battle-cdb90-matching-review-2026-09-21.md) explains duplicate sectors, combined battles, chronology conflicts and known source errors. Every source row has a disposition in [the intake report](../../data/reports/battle-cdb90-intake.json). A failed approved row blocks publication without replacing the previous profile file. Reviews are bound to the same source revision; the offline catalogue checker verifies artifact digests and approved profile sets.

The [article-alias review](../../docs/battle-cdb90-article-alias-review-2026-09-22.md) permits a different published English title only through an explicit, dated, sourced `articleAliasReview` bound to the original source title, target title and QID. It does not follow redirects or match similar names automatically. The source DBpedia title and all other eligibility gates remain exact; alias evidence never becomes numerical provenance.

The pure Python adapter uses the full active-period date extent, never the source midpoint. An unknown hour ending at next midnight is not an extra battle day. Initial and total-engaged strengths are compared only when their accounting is compatible; averages stay excluded. Original `NAM` force designations preserve coalitions even when derived `actors` are incomplete. A source-backed `identityReview` may supply a clearer localized name for a local force; its evidence attaches to participant identity. The original name, IDs, side keys and quantities remain unchanged, and reused participant IDs cannot be renamed through this mechanism. Signed uncertainty percentages remain independently optional: a zero margin means unknown, while an explicit zero casualty count remains zero. `cas` produces personnel casualties, never inferred deaths. Historical source estimates are not independently certified counts; known force-scope and side-allocation conflicts are held outside the import. Equipment is applied afterward from its own evidence, so rerunning the import cannot absorb an equipment review into CDB90 provenance.

Manual quantitative reviews cover Waterloo, Gettysburg, Trafalgar, Hastings, Isandlwana, Cannae, Kursk, Agincourt, Plassey, Antietam, Rorke's Drift, Marathon, Sekigahara, Yarmouk, Cape Ecnomus, Culloden, Quebec, Bunker Hill, Trenton, Cowpens, Lake Erie, Shiloh, Hatteras–Alabama and Havana. The [quantity source audit](../../docs/battle-evidence-review-2026-09-21.md) and [additional naval reviews](../../docs/battle-equipment-america-asia-review-2026-09-21.md) explain the scope and unresolved evidence. Reviewed army names include English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese translations; Chinese and Russian interface selections currently use the available name fallback.

Cannae retains Polybius' ancient estimates and marks Roman infantry deaths as a subset, rather than a whole-army death toll. Kursk figures describe the opening forces on 5 July 1943, excluding subsequent reinforcements. Isandlwana preserves both the museum's approximate Zulu strength and its separate lower-bound statement. Agincourt follows Anne Curry's archival reconstruction with an explicitly identified midpoint for the English range; incomplete lists of named deaths do not become a total. Plassey's force sizes include contingents which did not fight, and the Nawab's losses remain a lower bound. Antietam retains whole-army strengths including reserves and separately sourced casualty categories. Rorke's Drift includes hospital patients in the garrison count; qualitative reports of wounds do not become invented numerical losses. A lack of comparable losses never means zero losses.

Marathon and Yarmouk explicitly identify their reviewed secondary estimates from World History Encyclopedia. Marathon separates Athenian and Plataean hoplites from Persian fighting infantry; uncertain cavalry evidence stays unassigned, as do Herodotus' foreign deaths which cannot be allocated to infantry alone. Sekigahara uses the National Park Service's initial force estimates and keeps its overall death estimate in `totals`, without inventing per-side losses. Profiles may supply `totals` and `unassigned` evidence alongside participants; the build preserves those quantities and their references. An explicit source classification as `city-state` or `polis` identifies a political participant; a generic city does not establish an army or a belligerent side.

Explicit military-branch and armed-forces classifications also identify military participants. Historical unrecognized states and states with limited recognition remain political participants when the selected source claims establish that type. Preferred and nondeprecated claim rules still apply, and people remain separate from armies. Participant references include the participant entity revision as well as its appearance in the event. Source classification cannot repair an anachronistic identity: those corrections require a separate reviewed profile.

`data/curated/battle-equipment.json` adds equipment-only reviews without creating force sizes, casualties or opposing camps. Each participant can specify its own medium, allowing naval and shore forces in the same scene. Ship counts, soldier counts and aircraft counts have separate scales; a source's human casualties cannot remove ships. Event-level equipment applies only to an anonymous illustration. The catalogue filters use a record's naval or air medium even when its broader source type is simply `battle`.

## Verification

`pnpm exec vitest run tests/unit/battle-data.test.ts` checks units, uncertainty, provenance and the separation of humans from armies. The build validates every complete record and the index using `lib/battles/schema.ts`. `pnpm data:battles:check` validates the published catalog without needing raw data, checks each candidate's classification, and requires complete acquisition metadata. It compares every index field with its record, rejects duplicate participants, verifies that documentation badges represent opposing camps with comparable units, and recomputes the coverage report's quantities and missing-data reasons. It also checks every existing battle’s dates and coordinates against the original event corpus. A difference requires an explicit metadata review whose before-values match the original and whose corrected values match the output. The verification report lists these exceptions; the original event files are read without alteration.

`pnpm exec tsx pipeline/battles/readiness.ts` writes `data/reports/battle-readiness.json` for every published record using the actual `buildBattleSimulation`, `resolveUnitProfile` and `unitLossState` functions. It distinguishes mapped records, identified armies and camps, comparable versus complete force counts, compatible loss evidence versus visible removed models, and complete/partial/unknown/date-incompatible equipment. Equipment attributed to a reviewed army differs from a generic ship or aircraft silhouette. It checks referenced model files and reports combined coverage at both focused desktop and mobile budgets. Input hashes identify the corpus and code measured. This is a data-readiness audit, not proof of exhaustive history, surveyed locations, correct browser rendering or task completion.

The regenerated report covers all 23,597 records with focused budgets of 80 desktop and 36 mobile figures. All 16,828 original atlas battles remain in the independent catalogue; Eckmühl has an explicit reviewed coordinate correction; Herbsthausen, Mukden, Festubert, Third Isonzo and First Gaza have reviewed date corrections. No candidate is missing. The remaining 6,750 records lack a usable date or location. There are 195 participant profiles (36 manual and 159 CDB90 imports), 142 equipment-only reviews and twelve metadata reviews; these sets overlap.

Across all records, equipment resolves as complete for 2,110, partial for 713, unknown for 16,265, incompatible with the date for 14, and missing a date for 4,495. The full asset catalogue contains 58 GLBs and 63 profiles, including scoped aliases, totalling 19,285,788 bytes and loaded per scene.

It does not infer complete army composition.

`equipmentIdentity` associates a source-reviewed local military unit with a polity only for eligibility of an explicitly requested profile. It preserves the local army ID, quantities and opposing camp; it does not infer a model from identity alone or bypass date/medium gates.

`data/curated/battle-metadata.json` supplies narrowly reviewed start/end/location corrections. The [metadata audit](../../docs/battle-metadata-review-2026-09-21.md) documents three newly mapped records and Eckmühl’s correction from Munich to the documented castle fighting sector. Exact before-values block stale reviews; year/month precision remains intact, provenance is published, and coordinate basis is visible in the panel. A metadata entry may supply its own ISO-calendar `reviewedAt`; otherwise the file-level date is retained. Adding a new review therefore need not redate existing corrections. No troop positions or movement paths are inferred from a landmark point.
